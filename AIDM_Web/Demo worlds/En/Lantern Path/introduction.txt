You have taken the lantern-keeper's route through the woods. Before dusk you must walk the Path, light the lanterns, and make sure none has gone out. A cart of apples will come along this road in the evening, and without the lights the driver will turn the wrong way.

Someone is already waiting at the first post. The old lantern glows by itself, though you have not poured any oil yet. A fox in a checked waistcoat sits on the post, reading a tiny book and holding it up with one paw.

"This lantern is my library," he says politely. "It must not be put out. It may be moved, if you ask. My name is Wick. Farther on, the path remembers people who say hello."

Around the bend a ferry bell is ringing: the river has to be crossed before the ferryman goes off to his evening tea. Your bag holds an oil can, matches in a tin, and a list of nine lanterns. Only the first one is lit.

Wick closes the book.

"Will you come with me as far as the ferry, or check the second post first?"
